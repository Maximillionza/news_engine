# Tier 1 methodology

Plain-text export of the POC sheets in `docs/News_Engine_Causation_Matrix_v2.xlsx` (the source of truth for the research evidence). Do not hand-edit: change the workbook or `SECTIONS` in `scripts/export_tier1_methodology.py`, then regenerate with `python scripts/export_tier1_methodology.py` (`--check` exits 1 when this file is out of date). Which titles are covered, and their tier, comes from `TIER1_EVENTS` in `data_layer/tier1_events.py`. The workbook rows below are the sheet's own words, unedited.

Tiers: **formula** = a deterministic rule on a published model output, captured by the Windows tasks (CPI m/m, Core CPI m/m, CPI y/y, Core CPI y/y, Core PCE Price Index m/m); **judgment** = researched by hand before each release by the Tier 1 research task (`scripts/tier1_research.py`), where the first pre-release call stands and a later re-check is only logged as a late check; **market-implied** = a fixed rule on a traded market's prices, captured by the same Windows tasks (Kalshi: PPI m/m, Non-Farm Employment Change, Federal Funds Rate, Advance GDP q/q, ISM Manufacturing PMI, Retail Sales m/m). One producer per title: the research task refuses formula and market-implied titles. A judgment row whose source mentions FedWatch, or whose value opens with a FedWatch citation, also displays as market-implied (its call is the futures-implied rate path, not a researcher's view); a mere mid-text mention of FedWatch stays judgment.

Formula rule (the five formula titles): `scripts/capture_nowcast_from_cleveland.py` compares the Cleveland Fed nowcast with the release's forecast. The m/m titles read `nowcast_month.json` (month-over-month, seasonally adjusted); CPI y/y and Core CPI y/y read `nowcast_year.json` (year-over-year, based on not seasonally adjusted CPI and core CPI data, the basis BLS publishes y/y on). Within one rounding step of the forecast's own published precision is in line (neutral call); above is hotter, below is cooler. Each tracked instrument's direction comes from `webapp.stats_methods.market_lean` (buy -> bullish, sell -> bearish, mixed -> neutral). Confidence is always "Likely" and the source is "Cleveland Fed nowcast (formula)". At most one row is written per release and instrument per distinct nowcast value, and no readable forecast writes nothing (never a guessed neutral).

Market-implied rule (the six Kalshi titles): `scripts/capture_tier1.py` reads the open "above strike" ladder of the Kalshi event that resolves on the release (public read-only API; KXPAYROLLS, KXUSRETAIL, KXUSPPI, KXISMPMI, KXGDP, KXFED) and applies `webapp/tier1_market.py`. Only liquid strikes count: open interest >= 10, a quoted ask, and ask - bid <= 0.25. Each strike's yes midpoint is P(print above the strike), made non-increasing in the strike by a running minimum. The implied median is where that curve crosses 50%: linear between the two strikes around the crossing, or the first strike at or below 50% for the discrete Fed Funds ladder (KXFED; rates move in 25 bp steps, so interpolating a priced hike would land between two possible outcomes). The median is compared with the forecast with the same one-rounding-step tolerance as the formula rule (hotter / in line / cooler), and each tracked instrument's direction comes from `market_lean` with the series' own surprise sign (higher = USD-bullish for all six). Confidence is always "Likely" and the source is "Kalshi market-implied". At most one row is written per release and instrument per distinct implied median. No row (reported, never a guessed neutral) when the curve has no 50% crossing among liquid strikes, the forecast sits far outside the liquid strikes (a units mismatch), there is no readable forecast, or no open event matches the release.

**Rule:** Formula tier, not researched. The research task never logs these titles. The sheet's operating rule still frames the result: the nowcast is unreliable in the first 1-2 weeks of the month it covers and strong in the final 1-2 weeks before release.

## POC_PPI_Leading_Indicators

Covered titles: `PPI m/m` (market_implied)

**Rule:** Market-implied tier, not researched. The call is the Kalshi KXUSPPI ladder (ticketed by the release date, +/-1 day; strikes in the print's own unit) read by the market-implied rule in the introduction and written per instrument by the Windows tasks. The workbook's method below (BLS's own most recent prior PPI release read through the Final Demand-Intermediate Demand stages, plus the most recent ISM Manufacturing Prices Paid reading and the 5-district regional Fed prices-paid average one notch below it) is context for reading a miss, not an input to the call. The research task never logs this title.

**Rejected or not verified:**

- PPI as a lead for CPI: the release-timing lead is certain, but the strength and lag of the pass-through rest on a secondary source only (Tier 2).
- A single region's prices-paid print on its own: noisy; only the 5-district average is used.

**Confidence rules:** Always "Likely" for market-implied rows: a traded price is a crowd's bet, not a certainty. No 50% crossing among liquid strikes, a units mismatch or no readable forecast writes no call at all.

**Workbook rows** (verbatim):

- **Tier 1 - Verified core** — PPI's own Crude Materials and Intermediate Demand sub-indices (same monthly release as the headline): BLS's Final Demand-Intermediate Demand (FD-ID) system was built specifically to track price pressure moving through the production chain: crude materials -> intermediate demand -> final demand (the headline number). This is a DIFFERENT mechanism type than CPI's - an internal structural lead within the same report, not a separate external market-price series (Lag: Same release, but crude/intermediate stages are conceptually upstream of final demand by the system's own design; Confidence: Certain - a BLS economist (Jonathan Weinhagen) published a study in BLS's own Monthly Labor Review specifically testing 'the causal direction of price change' within this system and found 'evidence of forward price transmission in both the commodity-type and production-flow portions' - empirically tested by the primary source itself, not assumed; Evidence: BLS Monthly Labor Review 2016, 'Price transmission within the Producer Price Index Final Demand-Intermediate Demand aggregation system' (bls.gov/opub/mlr/2016); BLS transitioned from the older Stage-of-Processing system to FD-ID in 2014 specifically for this purpose)
- **Tier 1 - Verified core** — PPI's own release timing relative to CPI: PPI is released before CPI in the same month's cycle, which is part of why it is watched as an early signal - this is a scheduling fact, not a statistical claim, so it is trivially verifiable (Lag: PPI typically 2nd/3rd week of month, CPI shortly after; Confidence: Certain - confirmed against BLS's own 2026 release schedule already used elsewhere in this workbook (e.g. Dec 2025 CPI Jan 13 2026, PPI Jan 14 2026); Evidence: BLS 2026 schedule (bls.gov/schedule/2026), already cited in this workbook)
- **Tier 1 - Verified core** — ISM Manufacturing Prices Paid index (ISMPI): PROMOTED from Tier 2 after finding a real primary-source test. A Cleveland Fed Economic Commentary paper directly assessed this index's forecasting value and states: 'the ISMPI has some predictive content for producer prices but NOT for consumer prices' - a real, tested finding, not a common claim left unverified. A separate historical note quantifies it further: a 79% correlation over 20 years between the ISM prices-paid index and the 3-month change in PPI for intermediate goods specifically - which lines up with Tier 1's own intermediate-demand stage above (Lag: ISM Prices Paid for month M releases on the first business day of month M+1 (e.g. July's reading published Aug 3); that SAME month's PPI releases roughly mid-month-plus-one (July PPI published Aug 13) - so ISM leads that month's own PPI by about 10 days, not the following month's PPI. NEGATIVE FINDING WORTH KEEPING: the same paper found no such predictive value for CPI - if this index is ever considered for CPI's Tier 2, this finding argues against it, not for it; Evidence: Bognanni, Mark & Young, Tristan, 2018, 'An Assessment of the ISM Manufacturing Price Index for Inflation Forecasting,' Economic Commentary, Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland, 2018(05), DOI 10.26509/frbc-ec-201805; Haver Analytics archive note on the 79% ISMPI/PPI-intermediate-goods correlation)

- **Tier 2 - Plausible, PARTIALLY supported** — PPI as a leading indicator for CPI (cross-event link, relevant to CPI's own Tier 2 list): PPI's release-timing lead over CPI plus a documented (if imperfect) producer-to-consumer price pass-through mechanism (Lag: 'Several months later', per secondary source - not a primary-source-quantified lag; Confidence: Guessing to Likely - the TIMING lead is Certain (see Tier 1 row above), but the STRENGTH and LAG of the pass-through itself is only sourced to a secondary glossary description ('pass-through is imperfect and depends on producer pricing power and demand conditions'), not a primary BLS/Fed study the way the FD-ID transmission was. - **Tier 3 - Ad hoc/qualitative conditioning (shared with CPI's taxonomy)** — Geopolitical war-driven oil supply shock: Even more directly mechanical for PPI than for CPI - crude materials and intermediate energy inputs are PPI sub-index components themselves, not just a pass-through channel (Lag: Immediate; Confidence: Certain - same real event as CPI's Tier 3 row (Feb 28 2026 Iran war), applies here with an even more direct structural link since PPI's crude-materials index IS a commodity-price index; Evidence: Same sourcing as the Layer 1 / CPI POC entry for this event)
- **Tier 3 - Ad hoc/qualitative conditioning** — Tariff/trade policy actions: PARTIALLY RESOLVED via cross-reference to CPI's now-quantified finding: the NY Fed/NBER pass-through study's INDIRECT effect (36% of consumer pass-through, 9-12 month lag) is specifically the mechanism of domestic producers facing higher input costs and raising markups - this IS a PPI-relevant channel even though the paper measures the consumer-price outcome, not PPI directly. ## POC_NFP_Leading_Indicators

Covered titles: `Non-Farm Employment Change` (market_implied)

**Rule:** Market-implied tier, not researched. The call is the Kalshi KXPAYROLLS ladder, ticketed by the reference month (the month before the release: KXPAYROLLS-26OCT settles on the October report released in November); its strikes are listed in jobs and divided by 1000 to match the forecast's "K". The market-implied rule in the introduction turns it into the call. The workbook's method below (initial jobless claims read against their own recent trend, turning roughly 2-4 months ahead of the unemployment-rate cycle) is context for reading a miss, not an input. The research task never logs this title.

**Rejected or not verified:**

- ADP National Employment Report as an "NFP preview": REJECTED (City Index 2007-2024 backtest: 47.7% directional hit rate, worse than a coin flip; ADP counts a different construct from BLS payrolls).
- Continuing claims, JOLTS job openings, Challenger job cuts: Tier 2, not verified; not usable as live inputs.
- Large corporate layoff waves: no real in-window instance identified yet.

**Confidence rules:** Always "Likely" for market-implied rows. The claims evidence below never raises or lowers it; it explains a miss after the print.

**Workbook rows** (verbatim):

- **Tier 1 - Verified core** — Initial jobless claims, read against a TIME-VARYING threshold (not a fixed number): Claims capture layoffs directly - firms cut staff when business prospects worsen, making claims a real leading signal of labor-market direction. The key methodological point: a FIXED threshold (like the commonly-cited '400,000') is crude; a time-varying benchmark that adapts to the current labor-market baseline is what actually improved forecast accuracy in real testing (Lag: Weekly (highest-frequency labor-market data available) vs unemployment rate's monthly release - claims typically turn 2-4 months ahead of an unemployment-rate cycle inflection, per practitioner-level analysis (not the primary source's own stated lag figure, kept separate in confidence below); Confidence: Certain (the threshold-deviation methodology itself, out-of-sample tested) / Likely (the specific '2-4 months' lag figure, sourced to a practitioner blog, not the academic paper itself); Evidence: Braxton, John Carter, 2013 (rev. 2014), 'Revisiting Initial Jobless Claims as a Labor Market Indicator,' Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City, RWP 13-03: introduces a time-varying claims threshold and shows via an out-of-sample forecasting experiment that the deviation from it 'can improve forecasting accuracy' of the unemployment rate)
- **Tier 2 - Plausible, NOT yet verified** — Continuing jobless claims: Measures how long people STAY unemployed once laid off (duration), complementing initial claims' layoff-flow signal (Lag: Weekly, lags initial claims by construction (must first file initial, then continue); Confidence: Guessing - real and commonly cited, but no primary correlation/forecast-improvement study located this pass; Evidence: Not sourced this pass)
- **Tier 2 - Plausible, NOT yet verified** — JOLTS Job Openings: Commonly cited as a forward-looking labor-demand signal (openings precede actual hires) (Lag: Monthly, published with a roughly 5-6 week lag to the reference month; Confidence: Guessing - not sourced this pass; Evidence: Not sourced this pass)
- **Tier 2 - Plausible, NOT yet verified** — Challenger Job Cut announcements: Tracks announced (not yet effective) layoffs from a private outplacement firm's own tally (Lag: Monthly, released a few days before the Employment Situation report; Confidence: Guessing - not sourced this pass; Evidence: Not sourced this pass)
- **REJECTED - checked and found NOT to work, kept on record rather than silently omitted** — ADP National Employment Report ('NFP preview'): Commonly marketed and widely assumed to preview NFP, since it's a private payrolls report released 2 days before the official BLS release (Lag: 2 days lead - genuinely timely, exactly like ISM's 2-week lead on CPI was timely - but timing is not the same claim as accuracy, and this is the second time in this build that distinction has mattered; Confidence: REJECTED - a real backtest (City Index, 2007-2024 sample) testing whether ADP's month-over-month DIRECTION predicts NFP's direction found a 47.7% success rate - worse than a coin flip. A separate older source cites roughly 25% correlation between the two series. A third analysis of 36 months found only 8 fell within a 10% magnitude match, with 20-50% divergence being the MOST COMMON outcome. Real structural reason, not noise: BLS counts everyone actually paid; ADP counts everyone listed active on payroll - different constructs, not two measurements of the same thing; Evidence: City Index backtest analysis 2007-2024 (cityindex.com); Benzinga historical comparison (36-month sample); older correlation citation (~25%, TradingView-hosted commentary) - three independent sources converging on the same rejection)
- **Tier 3 - Ad hoc/qualitative conditioning** — Government shutdown / data-release disruption: Same mechanism already documented for CPI, but this is arguably where it was FIRST established in this workbook: the Oct 2025 shutdown row already states it delayed 'the September NFP report due that Friday' - a direct, already-sourced hit on THIS event type specifically (Lag: Event-driven; Confidence: Certain - already in Layer 1, just newly relevant to this POC specifically; Evidence: Same Oct 1 2025 shutdown row already in Layer 1)
- **Tier 3 - Ad hoc/qualitative conditioning** — Large-scale corporate layoff waves (e.g. a major tech-sector cut): A single sufficiently large announced layoff can move initial claims and Challenger's tally in a way that doesn't reflect broad economic conditions (Lag: Event-driven; Confidence: Guessing - no specific real in-window instance identified yet, flagged as an open item rather than invented; Evidence: Not sourced this pass)

This POC is deliberately built on DIFFERENT machinery than CPI/PPI's price-pass-through logic, per Masood's instruction that each event needs its own methodology - labor-market FLOW data (layoffs via claims), not price data. The ADP rejection is the most important finding here: a widely assumed 'NFP preview' indicator that real, repeated backtesting shows performs at or below chance for direction - structurally the same lesson as ISM's rejection for CPI. Not yet tested against a real NFP/unemployment print the way CPI and PPI were - that should happen once claims data around the next release is pulled and compared to the actual outcome.

## POC_FOMC_Rate_Decision

Covered titles: `FOMC Meeting Minutes` (judgment), `FOMC Statement` (judgment), `Federal Funds Rate` (market_implied)

**Rule:** Federal Funds Rate is market-implied, not researched: the Kalshi KXFED ladder (ticketed by the meeting month, KXFED-26DEC is the December decision; strikes are the upper bound of the target range), read with the discrete median (the first strike at or below 50%) and compared with the forecast. FOMC Meeting Minutes and FOMC Statement have no numeric market and stay judgment: CME 30-Day Fed Funds futures-implied probability (FedWatch methodology: implied rate = 100 - futures price; probability of a 25bp move = (implied rate - EFFR) / 0.25, day-weighted in the meeting month) against the current Effective Federal Funds Rate published daily by the NY Fed, cross-checked against the other covered releases (NFP, CPI, PCE, PPI, GDP), which are what move the futures. A judgment call that rests on FedWatch starts its source or value with "CME FedWatch" and displays as market-implied.

**Rejected or not verified:**

- Emergency / off-cycle Fed actions: the futures method cannot price them (a stated limitation of the method).
- Fed Chair transition effects on the reaction function: not quantified.

**Confidence rules:** Federal Funds Rate: always "Likely" (market-implied). Statement and Minutes: Certain when a clear futures-implied probability exists (a live, transparent market price, not a forecast that needs validating); lower it to Likely or Guessing when the implied probability is not clear-cut.

**Workbook rows** (verbatim):

- **Tier 1 - Verified core, but STRUCTURALLY DIFFERENT from every prior POC** — CME 30-Day Fed Funds futures-implied probability (FedWatch methodology): NOT a leading indicator that needs validating - a live, transparent, arbitrage-constrained MARKET PRICE, published with its own exact formula by the tool's creator. Implied Rate = 100 - Futures Price. Probability of a 25bp hike/cut = (implied rate - current EFFR) / 0.25, assuming uniform 25bp move sizing. For a month containing the FOMC meeting itself, the contract's implied average is DAY-WEIGHTED between the pre-meeting and post-meeting rate, since that single month straddles both regimes (Lag: Continuous/daily - updates with every futures trade, available at any point before a meeting, not just at scheduled release times like every other event type in this workbook; Confidence: Certain - this is CME Group's own published methodology for its own tool, about as primary as sourcing gets. ## POC_Sub_Event_Consolidation

No event titles: this sheet is guidance shared across events (see the rule).

**Rule:** Applies when sub-readings (headline vs core) point different ways for the one judgment title it still covers, Core Retail Sales m/m (read against headline Retail Sales m/m): the net direction follows the sub-reading with the larger surprise relative to its own typical volatility / forecast-distribution tightness, judged from real data, never computed. Not assumed to transfer to NFP. The formula-tier titles (CPI and Core CPI m/m and y/y, Core PCE m/m) and the market-implied titles (PPI m/m, Retail Sales m/m) each get their own rule call; the CPI cases below remain the evidence for the hypothesis.

**Rejected or not verified:**

- A fixed sub-component hierarchy ("core always wins") or a vote across sub-readings.
- Treating the hypothesis as validated: no genuine discriminating third case has been found.

**Confidence rules:** A working hypothesis (two real cases), not a settled rule: it decides the direction of a split, it never raises confidence; a split that cannot be judged from real data is logged Guessing with a neutral call.

**Workbook rows** (verbatim):

- **Tier 1 - working hypothesis, evidenced by two real cases** — Standardized-surprise mechanism: A series' net market direction is driven by whichever sub-reading (headline vs core, m/m vs y/y) produces the largest surprise RELATIVE TO ITS OWN TYPICAL VOLATILITY / forecast-distribution tightness - not by a fixed weight per sub-component (e.g. 'core always counts more') and not by a vote (e.g. '3 of 4 sub-readings leaned bearish'). A small deviation in a normally tightly-clustered measure can out-surprise a larger deviation in a normally noisy one (Confidence: Certain as a working hypothesis (evidenced by two real, decomposed, opposite-direction cases below); NOT YET Certain as a general rule - only twice confirmed, and professional practice sometimes still disagrees case to case (see Tier 2 caveat); Evidence: Derived from real market commentary across multiple unrelated sources independently describing surprise-relative-to-positioning as the actual driver, not headline-vs-core as a fixed hierarchy)
