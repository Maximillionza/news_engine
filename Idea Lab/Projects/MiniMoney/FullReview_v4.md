## Pass 1 — High-Level Review (from ExecutiveSummary.md only)

**What the Executive Summary reveals about the domain:** a real-money-
pegged financial-education app for minors (ages 6-18) in South Africa,
combining a payroll-simulation mechanic with gamified education content,
subscription-funded, built by a solopreneur on a thin budget and short
runway, with two unresolved South African regulatory questions
(SARB/National Payment System Act; FPB classification) and a live
child-psychology design trade-off (an exam-bonus mechanic with an
accepted residual intrinsic-motivation risk).

**Provisional panel assembled, with rationale (chosen fresh from the
domain signals in the Executive Summary, not from any existing roster):**

1. **Fintech/Payments Regulatory Expert** — the product touches
   real-money mechanics (invoicing, payslips, a pegged in-app currency)
   and two named unresolved regulators (SARB, FPB).
2. **Child Development/Psychology Expert** — the product is explicitly
   for minors 6-18 and has a named, accepted psychological residual risk
   (exam-bonus intrinsic-motivation crowding-out) and a late-payment
   penalty mechanic with family-stress implications.
3. **Data Privacy/POPIA Expert** — the product collects and processes
   children's financial and behavioral data under a parental-consent
   model.
4. **Venture Finance/Execution Analyst** — a stated R10,000 budget and
   6-month runway for a solopreneur venture is an unusual, load-bearing
   financial picture that needs independent scrutiny.
5. **Legal/Consumer Protection Expert** — debt-coded terminology,
   contractual capacity of minors, and advertising-code exposure are all
   named.
6. **Market/Competitive Analyst** — named competitors and a demand
   question (interview signal, pilot as demand vehicle) are flagged.
7. **EdTech/Curriculum Design Expert** — a 12-year age span (6-18)
   requires distinct instructional design, named as a domain extension.

**Provisional view: Genuinely uncertain, leaning cautious.** The
Executive Summary reads as an unusually disciplined, iterative document
(v18, seven required changes resolved this cycle) that is honest about
its own limitations — it names its own binding constraint (execution
capacity), retires an erroneous figure rather than hiding it, and states
plainly that demand validation will be directional, not statistical. That
level of self-scrutiny is a positive signal. But the summary itself
surfaces enough concentrated risk — a single person, R10,000, 6 months,
two open regulators, an accepted uninstrumented psychological risk — that
a provisional "Proceed" cannot be justified without checking whether the
full Business Case actually substantiates the mitigations claimed, or
merely documents the risks without resolving them.

**What Pass 2 needs to confirm or overturn:**
- Whether the "Complete" sections (per the case's own redefined rubric)
  represent real risk reduction or merely a made decision with the
  underlying work still undone.
- Whether any acquisition/CAC plan exists behind the funnel numbers.
- Whether the accepted exam-bonus motivation risk is actually observable
  in the adopted pilot instrumentation, or only asserted to be.
- Whether the legal foundation's one unverified citation is load-bearing
  for the case's strongest claim.
- Whether a contingency exists for what happens if the 6-month runway
  ends without external funding.

## Pass 2 — Full Review (from BusinessCase_v18.md)

### Expert 1: Fintech/Payments Regulatory Expert

- **Assessment.** The money-transmitter risk analysis (Legal & Compliance
  §1) is sound and Verified-tier, contingent on a locked product-spec
  constraint (Mbucks non-transferability). The two open regulatory
  questions (SARB/NPS Act, FPB — Legal & Compliance §6-7) each now carry
  an owner, a timing trigger, and a decided fallback, which is
  procedurally disciplined risk management.
- **Strengths.** Retained specialist legal opinion at Verified tier;
  clear NPS Act perimeter test citing the SARB/PASA joint communication
  and *Maize Board v Jackson* 2005 (6) SA 592 (SCA) for substance-over-
  form (Legal & Compliance §1).
- **Weaknesses.** Both rechecks are to be run by "the user personally"
  (Legal & Compliance §6-7; Roadmap), a non-lawyer, with no budgeted
  fallback for a paid follow-up consultation if either outcome is
  ambiguous — the professional-opinion policy's scope/limits are
  themselves undocumented (Assumptions, Financial Considerations).
- **Risks.** If the FPB recheck lands unfavorably mid-build, the
  no-Mpoints fallback's ripple effects (cosmetic store removal, 6-9 tier
  feedback-loop weakening, Google Play disclosure mooted — Legal &
  Compliance ripple trace) hit after engineering time is already sunk.
- **Opportunities.** Low money-transmitter risk removes the case's most
  catastrophic regulatory tail-risk (licensing).
- **Missing Information.** No budgeted reserve for a follow-up legal
  consultation if either recheck outcome is ambiguous.
- **Recommendations.** Earmark a small, explicit cash reserve (even
  R2,000-5,000) for a follow-up counsel touch-point tied to either
  recheck, rather than assuming the user's own reading suffices.
- **Confidence Level:** Medium.
- **Support Recommendation:** Proceed with Changes.

### Expert 2: Child Development/Psychology Expert

- **Assessment.** Five of six specialist design recommendations are
  fully adopted (Operations, Value Proposition, Curriculum Design). The
  sixth — the exam-bonus mechanic — is adopted as a hybrid that
  knowingly retains an outcome-contingent bonus layer against the very
  Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999) crowding-out evidence the case itself
  cites (Operations, Risks). The family-stress measurement package
  (PSI-SF/FAD-GFS borrowed items, CPRS Conflicts subscale, Success
  Criteria) is well-designed and correctly caveated as directional given
  n=20-50.
- **Strengths.** Three-tier age framework grounded in developmental
  literature (Jahoda 1981/Ng 1983, Curriculum Design); grace-period/
  pre-escalation mechanism reduces late-penalty trigger frequency at the
  source (Operations decision 1c); late-penalty remains fully invisible
  to the child (Objectives, Operations).
- **Weaknesses.** The case explicitly names a "measurement gap": the
  adopted pilot instruments target family stress, not the exam-bonus's
  accepted motivation risk (Success Criteria, "One measurement gap noted,
  not invented away"). A risk can be knowingly accepted, but calling it
  "observable qualitatively" without naming an actual instrument is a
  promise, not a plan.
- **Risks.** Intrinsic-motivation crowding-out in children tied to a
  real-money reward system is a durable, not merely transient, risk if it
  materializes — being taken on the case's own admission without direct
  measurement.
- **Opportunities.** If the hybrid performs as intended, it may
  out-compete a pure-behavior design commercially, since parents likely
  want outcomes rewarded too.
- **Missing Information.** No interview guide, debrief script, or
  qualitative protocol specified for capturing the exam-bonus motivation
  risk during the pilot.
- **Recommendations.** Add a lightweight qualitative motivation probe
  (2-3 open-ended questions to parents and age-appropriate children at
  pilot midpoint/end) to the existing instrumentation before pilot
  enrollment — near-zero marginal cost, closes a named gap.
- **Confidence Level:** Medium-High.
- **Support Recommendation:** Proceed with Changes (instrument the
  exam-bonus motivation risk before enrollment).

### Expert 3: Data Privacy/POPIA Expert

- **Assessment.** The universal parental-consent gate is confirmed
  legally sufficient under POPIA s34/s35(1)(a) (Legal & Compliance —
  Child Data & Consent, Critical, marked Complete). POPIA s14 retention
  is correctly named as an unresolved, affirmatively-required design task
  (Legal & Compliance §3; Child Data & Consent), not a closed legal
  question — the section is scored Complete under the case's own rubric
  because a *decision to design it* exists, not because it is designed.
- **Strengths.** No carve-outs in the consent gate; two named hardening
  tasks (consent-flow documentation separation; parent identity
  verification).
- **Weaknesses.** The identity-verification hardening measure's natural
  integration point (account-linking) is itself conditional on the SARB
  recheck (Legal & Compliance §6; Child Data & Consent) — a Critical
  section's hardening measure has an unresolved dependency chain running
  through a different Critical section's open regulatory question. This
  coupling is disclosed in two separate places but never named as a
  single compound risk.
- **Risks.** If the no-account-linking fallback triggers, a
  non-account-linking identity-verification route (e.g., card
  micro-authorization) must be designed from scratch under runway
  pressure — currently a phrase, not a design (Legal & Compliance §6;
  Roadmap).
- **Opportunities.** Doing POPIA compliance well early is a durable
  differentiator versus informal competitors.
- **Missing Information.** No data-breach/incident-response commitment
  anywhere in the case, despite real children's financial and behavioral
  data being collected starting at the pilot.
- **Recommendations.** Add a minimal breach-response commitment
  (even one paragraph) to Constraints/Roadmap before pilot families
  enroll.
- **Confidence Level:** Medium.
- **Support Recommendation:** Proceed with Changes.

### Expert 4: Venture Finance/Execution Analyst

- **Assessment.** This is the sharpest concern in the case, and the case
  names it as its own top Critical Gap (Critical Gaps §1; Financial
  Considerations; Constraints; Risks — "execution-capacity risk"). A
  R10,000 (~$550) budget — 45×-220× below the previously-cited agency
  range — plus a 6-month runway must contain the build (~3 months), a
  parallel curriculum workstream, an instrumented pilot, and two
  personally-owned regulatory rechecks, executed substantially by one
  person, with zero cash buffer if founder capacity fails (Financial
  Considerations; Risks).
- **Strengths.** Honest reconciliation against the prior agency estimate
  rather than a silent restatement; correctly identifies the runway, not
  the cash figure, as the true binding constraint.
- **Weaknesses.** No CAC estimate or marketing budget exists at all
  against the 18,000-61,000 install funnel (Revenue & Costs, "Still
  open") — a funnel with no named acquisition mechanism is an
  aspiration, not a plan. No break-even/P&L analysis exists (Financial
  Considerations). No post-runway funding-ask size or form exists
  (Financial Considerations; Outstanding Questions), meaning the case
  intends to fundraise at month 6 with only directional pilot evidence —
  timed by runway exhaustion, not by strength of proof point.
- **Risks.** Single point of failure: one person, five-plus concurrent
  workstreams; the case's own Risks section admits "slippage in any
  single workstream consumes runway directly." No contingency is stated
  for what happens if month 6 arrives without funding secured.
- **Opportunities.** If it works, the cost structure is extremely
  capital-efficient for the founder's own risk/reward.
- **Missing Information.** No CAC hypothesis or channel plan (even a
  zero-budget/organic-only decision, explicitly stated rather than
  silently absent); no sensitivity analysis for a low-end scenario (1%
  conversion, 18,000-install floor) against minimum operating costs
  (hosting, payment/aggregator fees if account-linking ships); no stated
  go/no-go rule for month 6 if funding isn't secured.
- **Recommendations.** Before build-spec lock: (a) name at least a rough
  CAC hypothesis/channel decision; (b) produce a simple runway-slippage
  sensitivity table for at least one workstream; (c) state an explicit
  decision rule for what happens if month 6 arrives without funding
  (pause, self-fund further, or close).
- **Confidence Level:** Medium.
- **Support Recommendation:** Gather More Information (CAC/acquisition
  plan and a runway-slippage contingency are material enough to require
  before further build commitment, not merely tracked as open items).

### Expert 5: Legal/Consumer Protection Expert

- **Assessment.** Minor contractual capacity is handled well via the
  "rights without obligations" doctrine (Legal & Compliance §4) —
  described in the case itself as its "strongest legal position."
  Terminology/optics risk is correctly identified as the single
  highest-optics-risk item and is now mitigated via parent-facing-only
  debt language (Legal & Compliance §5, decision 1a).
- **Strengths.** Multi-layered regulatory review (NPS Act, POPIA, ARB
  Code, CPA, FPB) — unusually thorough for a R10,000-budget venture.
- **Weaknesses.** The Conradie v Rossouw pinpoint citation — which
  underpins the case's "strongest legal position" claim — remains
  unverified, carried as a pending external action with the retained
  lawyer (Legal & Compliance §4; Critical Gaps §8).
- **Risks.** No ARB ruling addresses this exact fact pattern —
  MiniMoney "would be a natural first test case if challenged" (Legal &
  Compliance §5). That is a nontrivial reputational/legal-cost exposure
  for a solopreneur with no legal defense budget.
- **Opportunities.** If MiniMoney becomes the test case and the position
  holds, it establishes defensible precedent ahead of competitors.
- **Missing Information.** No estimated legal-defense budget or informal
  complaint-response plan if ARB or FPB challenges arise post-launch.
- **Recommendations.** Treat Conradie v Rossouw verification as a hard
  gate before any public or marketing use of the legal opinion (already
  tracked as Critical Gap 8 — reinforce it is non-negotiable).
- **Confidence Level:** Medium-High.
- **Support Recommendation:** Proceed with Changes.

### Expert 6: Market/Competitive Analyst

- **Assessment.** Market sizing rests entirely on the user's own
  "lowest-confidence" funnel estimate (Market & Competition), with the
  n=10 interview signal correctly and repeatedly labeled as non-
  representative (Problem; Supporting Evidence). The 20-50 family pilot
  is explicitly acknowledged as statistically underpowered for demand
  validation (Validation Strategy) — meaning at the next funding decision
  point, no statistically valid demand evidence will yet exist.
- **Strengths.** Honest retirement of the erroneous 360-2,440 subscriber
  range rather than leaving inconsistent figures in the document (Market
  & Competition); genuine, quantified competitive differentiation against
  MoneyTime SA (R719.88/year vs. R995/year).
- **Weaknesses.** No structured market-sizing study exists anywhere in
  the plan (Critical Gaps §4). The schools-partnership channel — named
  as "the strongest demonstrated reach model in the category" — has no
  timeline, target, or resourcing plan (Market & Competition; Roadmap).
- **Risks.** If the direct-to-consumer funnel underperforms (plausible
  given the absence of any CAC budget, per the Finance expert), the one
  channel shown to work in this category is unaddressed and would
  require new B2B2C capability the solopreneur does not currently have.
- **Opportunities.** The payroll-simulation differentiation is real and
  coherent; no named SA competitor directly replicates it.
- **Missing Information.** No unit-economics benchmark from any
  comparable, even directionally (e.g., MoneyAfrica Kids' unpublished
  premium price is a named open item).
- **Recommendations.** Name at least one low-cost organic acquisition
  hypothesis, and instrument pilot recruitment well enough to derive a
  rough retroactive CAC signal.
- **Confidence Level:** Low-Medium.
- **Support Recommendation:** Gather More Information on demand/
  acquisition — not independently blocking, since the case has already
  disclosed and scheduled this limitation rather than hidden it.

### Expert 7: EdTech/Curriculum Design Expert

- **Assessment.** Curriculum content does not yet exist (Critical Gaps
  §6; Curriculum Design, Partial). The three-tier age framework is
  well-grounded in developmental research (Jahoda 1981/Ng 1983), but its
  reconciliation with the product's six-way sub-bands is explicitly
  flagged as still not done — a gap the case notes was already flagged by
  a prior review cycle and remains open.
- **Strengths.** The workstream is now committed to run in parallel with
  the build starting immediately (Roadmap, decision 5), rather than
  sequenced after — correct given curriculum sits on the critical path
  for the 30% engagement success criterion.
- **Weaknesses.** No instructional format, standards alignment, or named
  author/authorship plan exists (Outstanding Questions). A prior draft
  (`CurriculumDraft_v1.md`) is explicitly unreviewed, unadopted, and not
  an input to this cycle — effectively zero usable content exists today.
- **Risks.** Curriculum is the product's second core pillar alongside the
  payroll mechanic; without validated instructional content the product
  risks becoming "gamified chores" rather than education, undermining the
  7-of-10 interview respondents who cited interest specifically in the
  education aspect (Problem).
- **Opportunities.** Executed well, curriculum quality differentiates
  MiniMoney from apparently pure allowance/payroll-simulation
  competitors.
- **Missing Information.** No named author and no authoring cost exists
  — unclear whether the founder personally authors curriculum alongside
  build, pilot, and two regulatory rechecks, which would add a further
  concurrent workstream to an already-stretched solo founder.
- **Recommendations.** Name curriculum authorship explicitly (founder,
  contracted specialist, or AI-assisted drafting reviewed by the retained
  child-development professional) before further runway is consumed —
  this directly affects the execution-capacity risk named by Finance.
- **Confidence Level:** Low.
- **Support Recommendation:** Gather More Information — curriculum
  authorship must be named before feasibility within the stated runway
  can be assessed.

## Panel Discussion

**Consensus.** The case demonstrates unusually rigorous, iterative
self-scrutiny (18 versions; seven required changes from the prior review
cycle all resolved by genuine decision, not just documentation). The
legal foundation (money-transmitter risk, minor contractual capacity,
POPIA consent) is comparatively strong and Verified-tier. The single most
serious, case-threatening concern raised independently by multiple
panelists (Finance, Market, EdTech) is execution capacity: one person,
R10,000, 6 months, and five-plus concurrent workstreams (build,
curriculum authoring, pilot execution, two regulatory rechecks), with no
acquisition budget/plan and no stated action if the runway ends without
funding. Several panelists independently converged on the same
underlying pattern: things are "decided" at the policy level, but the
substance behind the decision (curriculum content, motivation-risk
instrumentation, non-account-linking identity verification, breach
response) often does not yet exist. The case's own tightened "Complete"
rubric is honest that Complete means "decision made," not "thing built" —
but that distinction needs to be actively carried forward, not lost, when
this case reaches Developing Committee.

**Disagreements.** The Finance expert wants "Gather More Information"
(CAC plan, runway contingency) before further build commitment; the
Child Development, Data Privacy, and Legal experts lean toward "Proceed
with Changes" since their respective gaps are addressable inside existing
workstreams without materially threatening the venture's viability. This
is a genuine split on severity: is the financial/execution gap
disqualifying-until-fixed, or addressable-in-parallel? The Market Analyst
is more forgiving of the demand-validation gap (already disclosed,
already scheduled directionally) than the Finance expert is of the
CAC gap (currently entirely absent, not even directionally planned).

**Trade-offs.** Speed versus rigor: proceeding into build now preserves
runway, but locks in decisions (exam-bonus hybrid, curriculum framework)
before some specialist-identified gaps (motivation instrumentation,
named curriculum authorship) are closed. Capital efficiency versus
resilience: the R10,000/solopreneur model is attractive precisely because
it is cheap, but the same design has zero shock absorption — a trade-off
the case names itself rather than hides.

**Alternative approaches.** Delay pilot enrollment by a few weeks to
close the two cheapest, highest-leverage gaps first (a motivation-risk
probe instrument; a named curriculum author) rather than proceeding
immediately. Consider a reduced-scope pilot (e.g., 10-18 only) to lower
the parallel curriculum-authoring load, deferring the 6-9 tier launch.
Consider seeking minimal pre-seed capital or a co-founder now, before
runway pressure peaks at month 6, rather than fundraising for the first
time under exhaustion pressure with only directional evidence.

**Remaining uncertainties.** Whether the SARB and FPB rechecks resolve
favorably, unfavorably, or remain unresolved by build-spec stage, and
whether the user can correctly interpret an ambiguous outcome without
paid counsel. Whether the pilot's directional demand signal, however
positive, will be sufficient for external funders at month 6. Whether
curriculum content, once authored, will hit the unbenchmarked 30%
engagement target. Whether the exam-bonus hybrid's accepted residual
motivation risk materializes in ways that damage the product's core
educational credibility.

## Investment Committee's Own Devil's Advocate

Built fresh from this panel's own reasoning above, not inherited from any
prior document:

1. **Is "70% readiness, no margin" meaningful, or is it grade inflation
   via redefinition?** The rubric's "Complete" definition was tightened
   this cycle specifically because a prior review found sections marked
   Complete while carrying undecided recommendations (Readiness Score).
   But under the new definition, sections like Operations, Risks, and
   Roadmap are Complete because a *decision was made*, not because the
   underlying thing was built or de-risked. Deciding to author a
   curriculum is not the same as having one. If rescored against "is this
   actually built or validated" rather than "is this decided," true
   readiness is meaningfully below the stated 70% — and arguably below
   the completion gate's own ≥70% threshold that decision-completeness
   alone was used to clear.
2. **Financial Considerations is scored a mere 2-point "Partial" despite
   the case itself calling execution capacity the "binding constraint"
   on the whole venture (Critical Gaps §1).** A rubric that scores its
   own top-named risk as a routine Partial, indistinguishable in weight
   from, say, an unbenchmarked success-criteria placeholder, undersells
   the severity of what is missing (no CAC, no break-even, no funding-ask
   sizing, no contingency).
3. **Is the exam-bonus decision actually "informed acceptance," or
   acceptance without ever looking again?** The case frames the hybrid's
   residual risk as a "deliberate, known-residual-risk user choice"
   (Operations). But the very instrumentation that would let anyone learn
   whether the risk materialized is absent from the pilot design (Success
   Criteria, "measurement gap noted"). An accepted risk with no
   measurement plan is not really accepted with eyes open — it is
   accepted and then not examined again until launch, at real children's
   expense.
4. **Is the schools-partnership channel under-resourced for institutional
   reasons rather than strategic ones?** It is repeatedly named as "the
   strongest demonstrated reach model in the category" (Market &
   Competition), yet it is the one channel with zero timeline, target, or
   resourcing anywhere in the plan — while five other workstreams compete
   for the same solo founder's time. If it is genuinely the
   best-performing channel, its absence from the roadmap deserves more
   scrutiny than a single "still unplanned" line.
5. **The 6-month runway is treated as a fixed fact, but it is simply the
   founder's own stated commitment, never stress-tested against the
   case's own list of five-plus parallel workstreams for one person.**
   Nothing in the case models what happens if the runway needs to be 8 or
   9 months instead of 6 — there is no scenario analysis for the
   assumption that most directly gates the case's entire timeline.
