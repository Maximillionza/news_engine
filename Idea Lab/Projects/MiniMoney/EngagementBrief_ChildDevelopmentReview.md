# Engagement Brief — Child-Development / Age-Appropriateness Review

> Prepared for external use: to be sent to a child-development
> psychologist or similarly qualified professional. Not a Panel or
> Vendor work product — compiled by the Chief of Staff directly from
> BusinessCase_v15.md and Verdict_v2.md for the user's own outreach.

## What MiniMoney is
A financial-education mobile app for children and teens aged 6-18
(South Africa), built around a deliberate "real job simulation": children
complete parent-assigned household/personal tasks and are paid a
real-money-pegged in-app currency, receive a "payslip" document
summarizing earnings and deductions, and the parent receives a
corresponding "invoice." The app also includes an academic-performance
bonus mechanic (a financial reward tied to improvement in school grades)
and, for the 15-18 age band only, an optional "Fintech Advance" module —
conceptual-only instruction (no real trading or execution) in
trending/entrepreneurial financial topics such as forex trading and
dropshipping, gated behind a separate, explicit parent opt-in.

Access to the entire app, including educational content, requires a
pre-existing, consenting parent account — there is no way for a child to
use any part of the app independently.

## The specific mechanic needing review
If a parent fails to mark a scheduled payment to the child as complete
on time, an escalating penalty (5→6→7 Mbucks/week of delay, capped
during pilot testing) is charged. By design, **this penalty is incurred
entirely by the parent — the child has no visibility into whether it was
charged or how much accrued.** This redesign (the penalty was originally
going to be visible to, or even affect, the child) was made specifically
in response to an earlier concern that the mechanic risked penalizing a
child for a parent's own administrative delay.

An independent review of this case concluded that while removing the
child's visibility narrows the concern, it does not resolve it —
whether it's a genuine risk reduction or simply relocates the risk to an
unmeasured channel (e.g. the child still experiencing indirect effects
of parental stress or household friction around the accumulating
obligation) has never actually been assessed by a qualified professional
in any version of this case.

## Specific questions requiring your assessment

1. **Terminology.** Is applying employment/payroll-adjacent language
   ("payslip," "invoice," "overtime," "late penalty," "arrears") to
   children as young as 6 developmentally appropriate, or does it risk
   normalizing labor-like or debt-like framing within a parent-child
   relationship? Does your answer differ meaningfully across the 6-18
   age span (e.g. is this a real concern at 6-9 but not at 15-18)?

2. **The redesigned late-penalty mechanic.** Given the parent-only,
   child-invisible design described above, does this meaningfully
   eliminate risk to the child, or can indirect effects (parental
   stress, household conflict, a child sensing tension without
   understanding its cause) still reach the child in a way this design
   doesn't account for?

3. **Age-band appropriateness of the core "real job" framing.** Should
   the payroll-simulation framing (payslip, invoice, "earning a wage")
   be presented differently — or restricted — for younger children
   (roughly 6-9) compared to older teens (15-18)? Is a 12-year age span
   too wide to treat with one consistent metaphor?

4. **The academic-performance bonus mechanic.** Tying a financial reward
   to improvement in school grades — any concerns about extrinsic
   motivation crowding out intrinsic academic motivation, or about
   financial pressure around school performance specifically?

5. **The "Fintech Advance" module (15-18 only).** Conceptual-only
   instruction in trending/entrepreneurial topics (forex trading,
   dropshipping) — no real trading occurs. Any concerns about
   introducing these concepts to older teens in a product otherwise
   built around real-money mechanics, even without actual execution
   capability?

6. **Recommended pilot metrics.** The case has a planned pilot (roughly
   20-50 South African families). What would you recommend tracking to
   detect family-relationship strain by child age band during that
   pilot — i.e., what would meaningful early-warning signals actually
   look like, beyond simple task-completion or dispute-rate metrics?

## What happens with your answer
This case is paused pending your input, alongside a separate legal
review. Your assessment will be incorporated into the next Business
Case revision — recommendations may lead to redesigning the late-penalty
mechanic further, adjusting terminology, or age-gating certain framing
differently across the 6-18 span, before any pilot testing with real
families proceeds.
