## 1. Child-Development Specialist (Retained; Expert Roster continuity from prior cycles)

This expert's outstanding brief is the review of the candidate
exam-bonus motivation-probe, whose trigger is defined (once a stable
working model exists, before any pilot testing with real families
begins — a pre-pilot gate, not a pre-build gate, confirmed unchanged this
cycle) but which has not yet actually occurred, and whose completion is
explicitly out of scope for v24. The specific assumption in play is the
case's own stated exam-bonus risk-acceptance assumption — that the
retained outcome-contingent results bonus's salience "will not dominate
the behavior-primary structure in the child's perception" — which the
Business Case itself labels untested and candidate-observable only via
this instrument. This specialist also carries the still-unreviewed
three-tier-to-six-way sub-band reconciliation drafted in the Curriculum
Design domain extension. A newly relevant point this cycle: this
specialist's own review, and the parallel Data-Privacy Practitioner
review, are two of the four items this document's own Constraints section
now explicitly names as unresolved-but-decided-category items — the same
pattern this document's Readiness Score section flags as under genuine
scrutiny given the funding-fallback finding. This expert should be aware
that "trigger defined" carries less institutional confidence going into
Developing Committee than it did in v23, and should expect the timing of
"once a stable working model exists" to be tested, not assumed.

## 2. Data-Privacy Practitioner (Retained; Expert Roster continuity from prior cycles)

This expert's outstanding brief is the review of the Incubator-drafted
candidate data-breach/incident-response commitment before real children's
data is collected at pilot — a review whose trigger is explicitly defined
(same pre-pilot, not pre-build, framing as Entry 1) but which has not yet
occurred, and whose completion is explicitly out of scope this cycle. The
specific assumption at stake is the Business Case's own admission that
the candidate commitment is "adequate as interim policy... pending" this
review, i.e., the document is currently operating on an unverified
assumption about the adequacy of its own founder-authored breach-notice
content (what constitutes a breach, notification timing, and recipients)
against POPIA Section 22's actual standard. This expert should also weigh
in on the still-unbuilt POPIA Section 14 retention-period/deletion-trigger
design and the consent-flow documentation separation, both named as
pending build tasks. The zero-marginal-cost advice-policy coverage for
this specific engagement remains confirmed, removing one potential
blocker to the review actually happening on schedule — the blocker that
remains is founder bandwidth, now demonstrably more contested given this
cycle's weaker funding-fallback finding (Entry 6), which increases the
odds the founder self-funds further rather than pausing for review.

## 3. Curriculum/Instructional-Design Specialist (Named, not yet engaged)

This expert remains unengaged, consistent with the curriculum
workstream's demand-gated deferral, but the case has a concrete,
quantified brief waiting: the v2-curriculum trigger is 500 paying
subscribers AND 60% 90-day retention, both required. The specific
assumption this expert would need to test is whether that dual threshold
is itself a sound product-validation signal — the Business Case names,
but does not resolve, the narrow question of what happens if one
threshold clears and the other does not (e.g., 500 subscribers with only
55% retention), which is exactly the kind of curriculum-launch-readiness
judgment this specialist's domain covers. This expert would also need to
resolve curriculum authorship (founder vs. engaged designer) and review
the Incubator-drafted three-tier-to-six-way sub-band reconciliation once
the trigger is met — none of which is time-pressured today, but all of
which remains genuinely open and is untouched by this cycle's findings.

## 4. Payments/Financial-Services Consultant (Named, informal input via retained counsel to date)

This expert's domain covers the two externally-unsettled regulatory
rechecks the user personally owns (SARB/NPS Act on account-linking; FPB
classification of Mpoints), both still pending at build-spec stage with
decided launch fallbacks. Beyond the regulatory rechecks, the specific
assumption most relevant to this expert's input is the case's own stated
2.0-children-per-subscribing-family conversion figure, applied to
translate the 6.1M reachable-child population into a 3.05M reachable-
family population — a figure the Business Case itself labels "not derived
or validated," applied uniformly to a subset (the reachable pool) that
may not share the population-wide average. This expert's judgment on
whether that conversion assumption is defensible would materially affect
confidence in the entire downstream revenue funnel (92-915 paying
families), which this cycle's founder-eligibility and acquisition-channel
findings left numerically untouched — the funnel figures are unchanged in
magnitude across many cycles now, a fact worth this expert's direct
scrutiny in its own right.

## 5. Growth/Acquisition Strategist (Named, not yet engaged)

This expert's brief centers on the case's single largest untested claim:
whether organic-only acquisition can plausibly reach the corrected
9,150-30,500 Year-1 family-install funnel. This cycle names four specific
channels (founder personal network/word of mouth; organic social media;
App Store Optimization; parent/community forums and groups) via
`Clarifications_v24.md`, replacing the prior generic "organic-only"
label — a real specificity improvement this expert should weigh
seriously. But the case study itself concedes, in the same clarification
that names the channels, that none carries a stated expected-volume
estimate. **The specific assumption this expert should test directly is
whether these four channels, in combination and without any paid boost,
can plausibly generate even the low end of the funnel (9,150 installs) in
Year 1** — a claim the case currently supports with adjacent-market
inference (GoHenry/Greenlight UK/US) rather than South Africa-specific
channel-performance data. This expert should also test the assumption
that word-of-mouth and organic app-store discovery alone can approximate
what MoneyTime SA achieves through 1,500+ school partnerships — a channel
this case has explicitly and repeatedly deferred for founder-bandwidth
reasons rather than tested as infeasible.

## 6. Nonprofit/Grant-Funding Specialist (Named; desk research delivered in v23, unfavorable founder-fact finding delivered this cycle)

This expert's relevance was established when `Verdict_v6.md`'s seventh
required change asked for a concrete non-dilutive funding target as the
fallback if the R20,000 self-fund ceiling is exhausted. Research House
Engagement 4 (`ResearchFindings_v4.md`) delivered a prioritized candidate
list in v23 — NYDA (primary), SEDA Technology Programme and Injini's
earlier-stage track (secondary), TIA (tertiary, uncertain fit). **This
cycle (`Clarifications_v24.md`) resolves the two founder-specific
eligibility facts the research itself could not, and the finding is
unfavorable: the founder's age (39) excludes NYDA outright, and
MiniMoney's current lack of company registration and SARS tax clearance
excludes SEDA as currently applicable.** This expert's brief now shifts
from "which candidates fit" to a narrower, harder question: is Injini's
earlier-stage cohort track (contingent on a future opening — none
currently open) or TIA (whose eligibility for a solo, non-research-
affiliated consumer-app founder was never confirmed by the desk research)
actually pursuable within the case's 6-month runway, and if not, what is
the realistic alternative if the R20,000 ceiling is exhausted before
either becomes viable. This specialist should also advise directly on
whether CIPC registration and SARS tax clearance — now a named,
unstarted prerequisite task for SEDA eligibility — are worth pursuing
proactively given the runway's own time pressure, a cost/benefit judgment
the case has not yet made.
