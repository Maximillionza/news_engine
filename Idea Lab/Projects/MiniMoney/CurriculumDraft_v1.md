## Age-Band Structure — Overview

Four bands are proposed, splitting the 6-18 span at points chosen for
plausible developmental and numeracy-capability breaks, not derived from
any child-development source consulted for this draft (see Gaps).

| Band | Ages | Working title | Core numeracy/financial capability assumed | Primary mechanic tie-in |
| - | - | - | - | - |
| 1 | 6-8 | Coins & Change | Basic counting, single-digit addition/subtraction, coin/note recognition | Mbucks as countable objects; "word sums" [Unverified-in-scope] |
| 2 | 9-11 | Earning & Saving | Two-digit arithmetic, simple percentages introduced conceptually | Task → Mbuck earning; first savings-goal concept |
| 3 | 12-14 | Budgeting & Planning | Percentages applied numerically, multi-step arithmetic | Payslip literacy; percentage-based expenses explained |
| 4 | 15-18 | Money Management (feeds into Fintech Advance) | Compound reasoning, multi-variable tradeoffs | Payslip → net-vs-gross; hands off into Fintech Advance [Unverified-in-scope] at 15 |

This is a proposed split only. It does not claim to match, and may
conflict with, whatever age-band boundaries are referenced elsewhere in
this case's history as an "existing design decision" (flagged as Unknown
in `BusinessCase_v15.md`'s Stakeholders section) — this draft was not
given access to that prior material and cannot reconcile against it.

## Band 1: Ages 6-8 — "Coins & Change"

**Learning goal:** recognize that money represents value, connect
completing a task to receiving something of value, and perform the
simplest currency arithmetic (adding small amounts, calculating change
from a purchase).

**Proposed topic sequence (indicative, not final):**
1. What is money? (physical coins/notes vs. the app's Mbucks as a
   stand-in for the same idea)
2. Counting Mbucks — matching a completed task to the number of Mbucks
   earned, with visual (not purely numeric) representation
3. "Word sums" [Unverified-in-scope] — simple narrative arithmetic
   problems ("You have 20 Mbucks. A sticker costs 12 Mbucks. How many
   Mbucks are left?"), introduced here as the age-appropriate entry
   point for currency and change-calculation reasoning, per the
   delegation brief's framing of this mechanic as already named for
   younger users
4. Earning vs. finding — the difference between Mbucks earned by
   completing a task and Mpoints (cosmetic currency) earned automatically
   alongside it
5. Saving a little — a simple, visual "savings jar" concept, not yet
   tied to a numeric goal or percentage
6. Introducing the payslip as a picture-based summary (not yet a literacy
   requirement — a parent-read-aloud artifact at this age)

**Instructional format recommendation:** short (2-4 minute), audio-
narrated interactive modules assuming pre- or early-reading literacy;
drag-and-drop or tap-to-count interactions rather than text input; bright
character-led presentation; immediate positive-feedback animation on
task/lesson completion, distinct from the Mpoints cosmetic-reward
animation so children can tell learning-feedback and earning-feedback
apart. No unsupervised text-entry fields at this band.

## Band 2: Ages 9-11 — "Earning & Saving"

**Learning goal:** connect the task → Mbuck → payslip loop to a concept
of accumulation over time, introduce the idea that some Mbucks are
"spent" (expenses) before the total is available, and begin simple
saving-toward-a-goal behavior.

**Proposed topic sequence:**
1. Reviewing the task → Mbuck loop, now with the payslip read directly by
   the child (early literacy assumed)
2. What is an expense? — introduced narratively first (e.g. a household
   bill), before the percentage-based mechanic is explained numerically
3. Simple percentages, conceptually — "10 out of every 100 Mbucks" as
   a physical/visual proportion (e.g. a 10-square grid), not yet a
   calculation the child performs themselves
4. Setting a savings goal — connecting Mpoints (cosmetic store) purchases
   to a "save up for X" narrative, distinguishing wants from needs
5. Reading a payslip — earnings, listed expenses, and the amount owed, as
   a literacy exercise distinct from the arithmetic itself
6. Introduction to "overtime" — extra tasks beyond the base budget, and
   why they increase the payslip total

**Instructional format recommendation:** short interactive lessons (5-8
minutes) combining light reading with visual aids; simple quizzes with
immediate right/wrong feedback and a brief explanation, not just a score;
first light use of a calculator-style widget within a lesson (not yet
required for gameplay). Gamified progress tracking (e.g. a visible lesson
"streak" or badge) is appropriate here and can be built to feed the
existing Mpoints cosmetic system rather than introducing a third reward
currency.

## Band 3: Ages 12-14 — "Budgeting & Planning"

**Learning goal:** perform the percentage-based expense calculation
directly, understand the payslip as a full accounting artifact (earnings,
expenses, overtime, net owed), and begin planning across more than one
pay cycle.

**Proposed topic sequence:**
1. Percentages, calculated — the child computes what a stated expense
   percentage removes from a given Mbuck total, mirroring the app's
   actual payslip math rather than a simplified analogy
2. Full payslip literacy — reconciling every line item (base earnings,
   overtime, itemized expenses, net amount owed) against the underlying
   task list
3. Budgeting across weeks — planning task effort against a savings goal
   that spans multiple pay cycles, not just a single payslip
4. Needs vs. wants vs. saving vs. giving — a four-way allocation exercise
   using the child's own recent (real, in-app) Mbuck totals as the
   worked example
5. What happens when a payment is late — introduced from the parent's
   side only, consistent with the late-penalty mechanic being parent-
   incurred and invisible to the child (`BusinessCase_v15.md`, Success
   Criteria and Value Proposition); this topic should explain the
   payslip/invoice relationship to the parent's bank payment without
   referencing the penalty mechanic to the child at all
6. Introduction to comparing prices/value — a light consumer-literacy
   module, not yet tied to any real purchasing feature

**Instructional format recommendation:** a mix of short video (3-5
minutes) and interactive worked examples; scenario-based quizzes with
branching outcomes (e.g. "you have 340 Mbucks and three goals — which do
you fund first?") rather than single-answer recall quizzes; the child
performs the percentage calculation themselves using an in-lesson
calculator tool, then compares their answer to the app's own payslip
calculation for that cycle as a self-check.

## Band 4: Ages 15-18 — "Money Management" (feeds into Fintech Advance)

**Learning goal:** treat the payslip as a real (if simulated) income
statement — including a first exposure to the general concept of
"net vs. gross" — and prepare the learner for whatever the separately-
scoped Fintech Advance module [Unverified-in-scope] introduces at this
age band.

**Note on scope:** the delegation brief for this draft states that a
"Fintech Advance" module is already scoped separately for ages 15-18.
This draft does not know that module's content (it was not made
available) and does not attempt to duplicate or design it. What follows
is the general financial-literacy content this band would need
regardless of Fintech Advance's specific scope, framed to hand off
cleanly into it rather than overlap it.

**Proposed topic sequence:**
1. The payslip as an income statement — net vs. gross framed generally
   (task earnings minus expenses equals net), without introducing real
   tax concepts the app itself does not model
2. Multi-cycle financial planning — budgeting toward a larger goal (e.g.
   a purchase months away) using historical payslip data the app already
   generates
3. Introduction to the idea of credit and debt, conceptually — borrowing
   against future earning capacity, explicitly framed as a bridge topic
   into whatever Fintech Advance covers in depth, not a replacement for it
4. Comparing value over time — a first, simple exposure to the idea that
   money today and money later are not equivalent (foundational only;
   not a compounding-interest calculation module)
5. Transition topic: how the MiniMoney mechanic (task-based, parent-
   funded, percentage-expense) differs from real-world earning
   (employer-funded, tax-withheld, market-rate), explicitly flagged to
   the learner as a simplification, not a literal preview of adult
   finances

**Instructional format recommendation:** longer-form content becomes
appropriate here (8-12 minute video or article-style lessons), self-paced
rather than gamified-quiz-driven, with optional discussion prompts a
parent could use offline. This band is the natural point to reduce
reliance on animated/game mechanics and increase reliance on real
worked examples drawn from the learner's own payslip history.

## Instructional Format — Summary Recommendation

| Band | Reading load | Primary format | Assessment style | Reward-system tie-in |
| - | - | - | - | - |
| 6-8 | Minimal (audio-led) | Interactive/animated, drag-and-drop | Immediate visual feedback only | Mpoints animation on completion |
| 9-11 | Light | Short interactive + light quiz | Right/wrong + brief explanation | Mpoints + visible lesson streak |
| 12-14 | Moderate | Video + worked interactive examples | Scenario/branching quizzes | Mpoints; self-check against real payslip |
| 15-18 | Full | Longer-form video/article, self-paced | Reflective/open-ended, optional | Minimal gamification; real payslip history as material |

General principle proposed across all bands: lesson completion should
earn Mpoints (the existing cosmetic currency), not Mbucks — keeping the
"real money" earning loop tied strictly to tasks, as already established
in the case, and using the cosmetic currency to reward learning
engagement without conflating the two systems the case has deliberately
kept separate.

## Cross-Band Mechanic Integration

- **Budget:** the parent-set budget is the numeric backbone for Band 3's
  percentage-expense exercises and Band 4's multi-cycle planning — both
  should draw on the child's own actual, current budget figures rather
  than invented example numbers, wherever the lesson engine can pull
  live data.
- **Tasks:** Band 1-2 content treats the task list as the entry point for
  every arithmetic example. Band 3-4 content treats the task list as
  historical data (a payslip's origin) rather than the object of the
  lesson itself.
- **Mbucks:** used consistently as the real-money-pegged unit in every
  band's worked examples, never abstracted into a separate "practice"
  currency — this draft assumes lesson content should always reference
  the child's real Mbuck mechanic, not a fictional stand-in, to keep
  the education and earning loops reinforcing each other.
- **Mpoints:** proposed throughout as the reward currency for learning
  completion specifically (see table above), distinct from Mbucks earned
  by tasks — this is a proposed convention, not something confirmed
  elsewhere in the case as already decided.
- **Payslip:** becomes a teaching artifact starting Band 2 (read-only)
  and a worked-example source starting Band 3 (calculated, then
  self-checked). The late-penalty mechanic is never surfaced to the
  child in any band's content, consistent with its parent-only,
  child-invisible design as established in `BusinessCase_v15.md`.

## Explicit Gaps Not Resolved by This Draft

This draft is a starting point, not a completed curriculum. It does not
resolve, and should not be read as resolving:

1. **Standards alignment** — whether any of this maps to CAPS or another
   recognized South African curriculum/financial-literacy standard is
   not addressed here at all; this requires a curriculum-design
   professional's input, not an Incubator draft.
2. **Content authorship** — this draft names a plausible topic sequence
   and format; it does not write actual lesson scripts, does not specify
   who would author or review them (in-house, licensed, or
   expert-commissioned), and does not estimate production cost or time.
3. **Age-band boundary validation** — the 6-8/9-11/12-14/15-18 split
   proposed here is a plausible, not a validated, developmental
   breakpoint. It may not match whatever age-band structure is referenced
   elsewhere in this case's history as an existing design decision (see
   Basis, above) — that material was not available to this drafting
   task.
4. **Child-development review** — nothing in this draft has been
   reviewed by the child-development/age-appropriateness expert this
   case has flagged as required since v9 and still not commissioned as
   of v15. That review, when it happens, may substantially change or
   reject the age-band splits and content proposed here.
5. **Fintech Advance content itself** — this draft explicitly does not
   attempt to design the 15-18 Fintech Advance module; it only proposes
   what should precede it.
6. **"Word sums" mechanic detail** — this draft accepts the existence of
   a "word sums" mechanic per the delegation brief and proposes where it
   sits in Band 1's sequence, but does not have access to whatever
   specific design already exists for it elsewhere in the case; it may
   duplicate or conflict with that prior design.
7. **Testing/validation of engagement** — no pilot, user testing, or
   engagement-benchmark data exists for any of this content (consistent
   with `BusinessCase_v15.md`'s Success Criteria section noting the 30%
   curriculum-engagement figure carries no external benchmark).
