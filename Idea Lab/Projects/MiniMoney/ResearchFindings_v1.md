## Item 7 — Data retention/deletion policy norms for child-directed apps under POPIA

**Original flagged item:** Data retention/deletion policy norms for child-directed apps operating under POPIA specifically (not GDPR-K or COPPA) — what do comparable South African child-directed digital products typically commit to?

**What I found:** POPIA's Section 14 establishes the general principle (not child-specific) that personal information may not be retained longer than necessary for the purpose it was collected for, and that destruction/deletion must render the record unreconstructable. POPIA defines a "child" as anyone under 18 and requires consent from a "competent person" (parent/guardian) for processing a child's data under Sections 34-35. A data subject (or their guardian, for a minor) has the right to request correction, deletion, or destruction of personal information at any time, and the responsible party has 30 days to respond and act. One example of a sector-specific retention norm was found: schools processing learner data are commonly cited as following a 7-year mandatory retention period post-enrollment before deletion can be requested — this is a schools/education-sector convention, not a general "child-directed digital product" norm, and was not confirmed to apply to consumer fintech or gaming-style apps for children. No specific published retention-period commitment from any named South African child-directed consumer app (MoneyAfrica Kids, MoneyTime SA, or others) was found in the search — none of their privacy policies were directly examined in this pass.

**Source(s):** [POPIA — Section 14 Retention and restriction of records](https://popia.co.za/section-14-retention-and-restriction-of-records/), [Michalsons — POPIA Regulations key updates](https://www.michalsons.com/focus-areas/privacy-and-data-protection/popia-regulations-in-south-africa-key-updates), [MyEncore — POPIA Compliance for Schools](https://www.myencore.co.za/news/popia-compliance-schools.html)

**Confidence in finding:** Medium — the general POPIA retention principle (Section 14) is High confidence (statutory text, well-documented); the 7-year schools convention is Medium (repeated across secondary sources but not traced to a primary POPIA regulation number); no comparable-product-specific retention commitment was found at all.

**Evidence tier:** Supported

**Could not resolve:** No direct evidence of what MoneyAfrica Kids, MoneyTime SA, or similar South African child-directed apps actually commit to in their own privacy policies regarding retention periods. Closing this fully would require a follow-up desk-research pass reading those companies' live privacy policy pages directly (a few additional hours, still desk research, no paid engagement needed) — this was not done in this pass as it fell slightly outside the original search scope and is flagged here as a concrete next step rather than left silently unaddressed. **Where:** Informs MiniMoney's own data retention/deletion policy commitments section. **When:** A few hours more desk research for the comparable-policy check; the underlying "is POPIA's general Section 14 principle sufficient without child-specific regulations (akin to COPPA's or GDPR-K's more prescriptive rules)" question is a legal-interpretation question, category: requires legal opinion, not resolvable by desk research alone (see Item 9 scoping below). **By how much:** Moves the general statutory framework from Unknown to Supported/High; the comparable-company-commitment sub-question remains Unknown; the "is this sufficient" legal-adequacy question remains outside desk-research scope entirely.

## Item 8 — Regulatory/consumer-protection precedent on "payslip"/"invoice"/"late penalty"/"arrears" terminology applied to minors

**Original flagged item:** Whether "payslip," "invoice," "late penalty," and "arrears" terminology carries any documented regulatory or consumer-protection precedent in South Africa when applied to a minor — i.e. has this specific terminology-to-minors question been addressed in any prior ARB ruling, National Credit Regulator guidance, or comparable consumer-protection context?

**Note per task order:** Not attempting a legal answer — surfacing what is publicly documented only.

**What I found:** No specific ARB ruling, National Credit Regulator guidance document, or other consumer-protection precedent addressing this exact terminology-to-minors question was found in the public domain. What was confirmed:
- The **ARB Code of Advertising Practice, Section II Clause 14**, defines "children" as under-18 and prohibits advertising that exploits their "credulity or inexperience" or that encourages children to pressure parents or undermines parental authority — this is a general principle that could plausibly be *argued* to apply to realistic financial terminology aimed at minors, but no ruling was found applying it to that specific fact pattern.
- The **ARB's Direct Marketing Advertising appendix (DMASA)** confirms that "marketing to children and minors imposes a special responsibility on marketers" — again a general principle, not a specific precedent on payslip/invoice/arrears-style terminology.
- The **ARB Section III (Specific Categories of Advertising)** covers financial/credit-product advertising generally (requiring disclosure of moneylender/broker status, ensuring consumers understand financial commitments) but this is aimed at adult consumers entering real credit agreements, not at simulated/gamified terminology used in an educational context for minors — no overlap with MiniMoney's specific use case was found.
- The **National Credit Act / National Credit Regulator** framework applies to actual credit agreements between real legal parties; a minor generally cannot enter a binding credit agreement in South African law, and no NCR guidance was found addressing simulated/educational use of credit-adjacent terminology in a non-binding, parent-controlled app context.

**Source(s):** [ARB — Section II General Principles v2025.1](https://www.arb.org.za/assets/section-ii-general-principles-v2025.1.pdf), [ARB — Appendix C Direct Marketing Advertising](https://www.arb.org.za/assets/lappendix-c_direct-marketing-advertising.pdf), [ARB — Section III Specific Categories of Advertising](https://www.arb.org.za/assets/l3.-section-iii-specific-categories-of-advertising-v2023.1.pdf), [National Credit Act 34 of 2005 — gov.za](https://www.gov.za/documents/national-credit-act)

**Confidence in finding:** High confidence that no specific precedent exists publicly (a clear negative finding); Medium confidence that the general principles cited are the closest applicable framework, since applying them to MiniMoney's specific terminology choice is an interpretive legal judgment, not a settled precedent.

**Evidence tier:** Supported

**Could not resolve — explicitly out of scope per task order:** No prior ruling or guidance was found addressing this exact question. This is confirmed to require a licensed South African attorney's interpretation of the general ARB/NCR frameworks against MiniMoney's specific product design — Research House is explicitly not attempting that legal judgment. **Where:** Informs the terminology-choice risk section of the Business Case. **When/category:** Requires a paid legal opinion (see Item 9 for cost-range scoping) — not resolvable by further desk research; a broader manual search of the ARB's full rulings archive (searchable by keyword per its own site) could be attempted in a follow-up pass but is unlikely to surface a ruling on this precise fact pattern given none turned up under multiple search approaches in this pass.
