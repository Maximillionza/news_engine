# Research Findings v4 — MiniMoney
## Research House Engagement 4 — Non-Dilutive Funding Fallback Options

Source task order: `TaskOrder_ResearchHouse_v4.md`. Self-contained
engagement — no BusinessCase file was read. All findings below are
**Supported tier** (external, sourced desk research). None are Verified
— that tier is reserved for facts the user has directly confirmed.

Two founder-specific facts are unknown to this desk research and gate
eligibility for several programs below: the founder's **age** (relevant
to NYDA's 18–35 band) and the founder's **race/gender** (relevant to
several BEE-linked or women-focused programs). Where a program's
eligibility depends on either, this is flagged explicitly rather than
assumed either way.

---

## Flagged Item 1

> "Named South African (or accessible-to-South-African-founders) grant
> or non-dilutive funding programs relevant to an early-stage,
> solo-founder, fintech-adjacent or edtech mobile app — e.g. innovation
> grants, sector-specific accelerators (fintech, edtech, youth-focused),
> government or quasi-government innovation funds (e.g. any relevant
> NEF, SEDA, or Department of Science and Innovation-linked programs, if
> applicable), or corporate/foundation-backed startup programs."

### What I found

Eight named, currently-traceable candidates were identified, split
between genuine non-dilutive fits and programs that initially look
relevant but fail on inspection. Full timeline/eligibility/award detail
for each is under Items 2–4 below; this section lists what exists and
what it is.

**Plausible non-dilutive fits:**
1. **NYDA (National Youth Development Agency) Grant Programme** —
   non-repayable grant, R1,000 up to a cumulative R200,000 cap (R250,000
   for technology or agriculture projects/cooperatives), for
   100%-youth-owned businesses.
2. **SEDA Technology Programme (STP)** — non-repayable grant up to
   R600,000 per project, administered via ICT-focused incubators,
   targeting startups including youth-owned businesses.
3. **Technology Innovation Agency (TIA) — Seed Fund / SMME Seed Fund /
   "Grant Startup Capital"** — several distinct TIA instruments,
   including one cited at up to R200,000 plus a R60,000/year project fee
   for locally-developed technology commercialisation.
4. **SAB Foundation Social Innovation Fund (Social Innovation &
   Disability Empowerment Awards)** — non-repayable grant funding,
   R300,000–R1.3 million range, for ventures addressing barriers faced
   by women, youth, rural communities, or people with disabilities.
5. **Injini (EdTech Accelerator, Cape Town)** — Africa's dedicated
   EdTech accelerator; earlier-stage cohorts (distinct from the
   Mastercard Foundation Fellowship below) have historically included an
   initial ~R100,000 grant component.
6. **FNB App of the Year** (formerly MTN Business App of the Year) —
   annual national competition with dedicated Education and Financial
   Services categories; historically carried roughly R1 million in total
   prize money across categories (2016-era figure; current-cycle total
   not independently reconfirmed — see Item 2).
7. **Google for Startups Black Founders Fund: Africa** — $50,000–$100,000
   equity-free cash plus Cloud credits, South Africa is an eligible
   country.
8. **Mastercard Foundation EdTech Fellowship** (delivered via Injini) —
   equity-free funding up to roughly R1,000,000 for the South Africa
   cohort.

**Named but not a genuine non-dilutive fit (kept in this list per the
task order's instruction to flag exclusions plainly, not omit them
silently):**
- **National Empowerment Fund (NEF)** — despite being explicitly named
  in the task order as a program to check, NEF's own public materials
  state it does not provide grant funding directly; its instruments are
  loans (from R250,000) and equity/quasi-equity, with grants occurring
  only rarely and in blended form. This is a dilutive/debt vehicle, not
  a fit for a non-dilutive fallback brief.
- **Standard Bank fintech incubator / Founders Factory Africa** —
  Standard Bank's named fintech startup relationship (with Founders
  Factory Africa) operates as an equity-investing accelerator, not a
  grant. Excluded on the same dilutive grounds.
- **Naspers Foundry** — the most prominent SA corporate venture vehicle
  of this kind wound down operations in March 2023 and no longer exists
  as an active fund; not a live option regardless of fit.
- **Grindstone (incl. the Naspers-partnered Grindstone X cohort)** — now
  positioned as a growth-stage accelerator for post-revenue, scaling
  businesses; the Naspers-partnered cohort specifically targets
  women-owned SMEs. Poor fit for a pre-revenue solo founder regardless
  of the founder's gender, given the growth-stage framing.
- **22 On Sloane's other named programmes** (Township Digital Market
  Access, Women Entrepreneurship Development, Microsoft SMME Capacity
  Building) — these target physical/township retail SMMEs or are
  explicitly women-only; no single named grant instrument at 22 On
  Sloane matched MiniMoney's profile as a solo-founder mobile app
  outside those categories, beyond the Google Black Founders Fund
  already listed separately above (22 On Sloane co-hosts a Google
  Startup Bootcamp tied to that same fund).

## Flagged Item 2

> "Realistic timelines for each identified option — application
> windows, typical time-to-decision, and typical time-to-disbursement.
> This matters specifically because the case's 6-month runway is tight;
> a program with a 9-month application-to-disbursement cycle is not a
> realistic fallback for a founder who has just exhausted a self-fund
> ceiling."

### What I found

| Program | Application window | Time-to-decision | Time-to-disbursement | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NYDA Grant Programme | Rolling, year-round via NYDA's ERP portal or branch offices — no fixed cycle | Not independently confirmed (varies by application volume/completeness) | ~30 working days after approval, per NYDA's own materials | Best-fit timeline for a tight runway among the programs found — no fixed-window bottleneck |
| SEDA Technology Programme | Via SEDA-appointed incubators; each incubator runs its own recruitment/intake cycle | Not centrally published | General SEDA programme timelines cited by a secondary source at 4–12 weeks, but this figure was not found specific to STP itself | Timeline confidence lower than NYDA's — the 4–12-week figure is a general SEDA claim, not STP-specific |
| TIA (Seed Fund / SMME Seed Fund / Grant Startup Capital) | Mixed — some TIA instruments run through periodic implementing-partner calls (e.g. an Eastern Cape call with a 31 Jan 2026 deadline was found, now closed); others appear to have rolling university/TTO-linked intake | One secondary source cited "on average, 3–6 months" generically, not attributed to a specific instrument | Not found | Weakest timeline evidence of the group — TIA's structure (Technology Readiness Level framework, historically research/TTO-oriented) makes it unclear whether a solo consumer-app founder even enters through a standard, predictable track |
| SAB Foundation Social Innovation Fund | Annual cycle; the 2026 cycle's applications closed 16 March 2026 — already past as of this engagement (July 2026) | Winners for the 2026 cycle were to be announced "later this year" (2026) | Not found | This cycle is closed; the next application window would plausibly open in early-to-mid 2027 on a similar annual pattern — not a usable near-term fallback |
| Injini EdTech Accelerator (earlier-stage cohorts) | Cohort-based, historically ~annual (Cohorts 1–4 tracked 2021–2024); no confirmed open 2026 cohort found for the earlier-stage track specifically | Not found for this track | Not found | Could not confirm whether a new earlier-stage cohort is currently open or planned in 2026 — flagged as unresolved below |
| Mastercard Foundation EdTech Fellowship (via Injini) | Annual; the most recent cohort's applications were confirmed closed as of this search | Multi-stage (form, virtual pitch/interview) | Not found | Excluded on eligibility grounds regardless (see Item 3) — timeline moot |
| FNB App of the Year | Annual; based on the pattern of the most recent cycle (2025 winners announced December 2025), entries likely open mid-year with judging into Q4 — exact 2026 entry-window dates could not be confirmed despite repeated attempts to load the program's own site | Judging appears to run through the second half of the year to a year-end announcement | Prize disbursed at/around the December announcement, per past-cycle pattern | Structurally a recognition competition for an already-built, in-market app (the 2025 winner had 2.5 million completed referrals at entry) — not designed as pre-launch seed capital |
| Google Black Founders Fund: Africa | Cyclical/cohort-based; specific 2026 South Africa window not confirmed in this search | Not found | Not found | Excluded on eligibility grounds regardless (see Item 3) |

several programs' own websites directly (TIA, FNB App of the Year,
Injini) for authoritative current-cycle dates timed out or returned
incomplete content during this engagement — this is a tooling
limitation of this research pass, not a confirmed absence of published
dates, and is flagged as a genuine gap below rather than papered over.

## Flagged Item 3

> "Eligibility fit — flag plainly where a program likely excludes this
> venture (e.g. requires existing revenue, requires a team of more than
> one, requires a different sector focus, requires South African company
> registration in a form not yet confirmed) rather than listing it
> without qualification."

### What I found

**Likely excluded or poor fit:**
- **Mastercard Foundation EdTech Fellowship** — explicitly requires
  "post-revenue, growth-stage" companies with annual turnover likely
  exceeding R1,000,000 and the potential to reach 8,000 direct learners
  within a year. MiniMoney, as described in the task order (pre-revenue,
  solo-founder, R20,000 total self-fund ceiling), does not match this
  profile. This is a hard, stated eligibility bar, not an inference.
- **Google for Startups Black Founders Fund: Africa** — requires "a live
  product in-market" with the founding team including at least one Black
  C-level founder or the venture directly supporting the Black
  community; explicitly excludes consultancies. The live-in-market
  requirement is a poor fit pre-launch. The founder-demographic
  requirement cannot be assessed here — this desk research has no
  information on the founder's race, and this is flagged as a fact only
  the founder can confirm, not assumed either way.
- **NEF, Standard Bank/Founders Factory Africa, Naspers Foundry,
  Grindstone** — excluded on structural grounds already detailed under
  Item 1 (dilutive, defunct, or growth-stage/team-scale mismatch).
- **FNB App of the Year** — not excluded outright, but poorly timed for
  MiniMoney's current pre-build stage: past winners in the profiled
  categories were live, adopted apps (e.g., 2.5 million completed
  referrals for the 2025 Education-adjacent winner), suggesting judging
  favors demonstrated traction over an early-stage concept. Realistically
  a later-stage option once MiniMoney has a live user base, not a
  near-term fallback for exhausting the R20,000 ceiling before launch.
- **SAB Foundation** — eligibility requires the innovation to be "beyond
  the idea stage" and "already being developed, tested or implemented,"
  which MiniMoney would likely satisfy once past initial build, but the
  2026 cycle is already closed (see Item 2), making this a next-year
  option rather than a current fallback.

**Plausible fit, with open eligibility questions that could not be
resolved from published material alone:**
- **NYDA** — requires the business to be 100% youth-owned and the
  founder(s) aged 18–35, South African citizens. Fit is entirely
  contingent on the founder's age, which this desk research does not
  know and does not assume. If the founder is outside this band, NYDA is
  excluded outright.
- **SEDA Technology Programme** — general baseline criteria include
  being a South African-owned and CIPC-registered business with a valid
  SARS tax clearance/compliance status. MiniMoney's current company
  registration form/status was not stated in the task order and is
  unconfirmed here; if unregistered as a formal entity, this would need
  resolving before applying. STP explicitly lists youth-owned businesses
  as a target group, which strengthens fit if the founder is
  youth-classified, but this is not a strict requirement the way NYDA's
  age band is.
- **TIA instruments** — the clearest open question of the group. TIA's
  funding language is built around Technology Readiness Levels and
  appears oriented toward research-derived or hard-technology innovation
  (its Seed Fund explicitly references university/TTO/research-council
  pathways). Whether a solo founder building a straightforward consumer
  mobile app (no novel underlying technology, no research-institution
  affiliation) would even be considered "technology innovation" in TIA's
  sense could not be confirmed either way from public material — this is
  flagged as a genuine unresolved eligibility question, not a likely
  fit or a likely exclusion.
- **Injini earlier-stage cohorts** — one secondary source suggested
  Injini "can help pre-product companies if feasible" but generally
  expects the concept to be "beyond the idea stage" with demonstrated
  demand signal — a real but softer bar than the Mastercard Fellowship's
  hard post-revenue requirement. Fit is plausible but not confirmed, and
  no currently-open cohort for this earlier-stage track was found (see
  Item 2 gap).

### Source(s)
Same as Items 1 and 2 above; no additional sources introduced.

### Confidence
Medium. The exclusions (Mastercard Fellowship, Google Black Founders
Fund's live-product requirement, NEF/Standard Bank/Naspers Foundry/
Grindstone) are drawn directly from each program's own stated criteria
and are High confidence individually. The "plausible fit, open
questions" group is genuinely uncertain — flagged as such rather than
rounded up or down.

### Evidence tier
Supported.

## Flagged Item 4

> "Rough typical award size, where publicly stated, so the founder can
> judge whether a given program is even proportionate to the
> R20,000+ gap being addressed."

### What I found

| Program | Stated award size | Proportionate to a ~R20,000+ gap? |
|---|---|---|
| NYDA Grant Programme | R1,000 up to a cumulative R200,000 cap (R250,000 for tech/co-op projects) | Yes — smaller NYDA disbursements in particular could match a modest gap; the program does not appear to require requesting the full cap |
| SEDA Technology Programme | Up to R600,000 per project (non-repayable) | Yes, and substantially oversized relative to a R20,000 gap specifically — but the grant is structured around specific allocations (e.g., cited 50%/80% co-funding splits for equipment vs. training/business-development costs), so the practical amount attributable to a pure cash-runway gap may be much smaller than the headline cap |
| TIA (various instruments) | One instrument cited at up to R200,000 plus a R60,000/year project fee | Plausibly proportionate if eligible, but eligibility itself is unresolved (see Item 3) |
| SAB Foundation | R300,000–R1.3 million per award (total pool R2.2 million across the 2026 cohort) | Oversized relative to the stated gap, and the current cycle is closed regardless |
| Injini earlier-stage cohort | Historically an initial ~R100,000 grant component | Oversized relative to the stated gap, but no confirmed open cohort |
| Mastercard Foundation EdTech Fellowship | Up to ~R1,000,000 (South Africa cohort) | Oversized, and excluded on eligibility grounds regardless |
| FNB App of the Year | Historically ~R1 million total prize pool across all categories (2016-era MTN figure); current per-category 2026 amount not independently reconfirmed | Not proportionate as a "gap-filling" mechanism in any case — it is a competition prize, not scaled funding |
| Google Black Founders Fund: Africa | $50,000–$100,000 equity-free cash | Oversized, and excluded on eligibility grounds regardless |

### Source(s)
Same as Items 1–3 above.

### Confidence
Medium-High for the headline figures themselves (each is stated on the
program's own materials or a closely-sourced secondary summary). Low for
whether any given founder would actually receive a proportionate slice
rather than the full or minimum award — none of the programs publish a
sub-cap "typical" disbursement figure, so all amounts above are ceiling
figures, not medians.

### Evidence tier
Supported.

## What I could not resolve

Stated plainly, per instructions, rather than stretched:

1. **No confirmed current-cycle FNB App of the Year 2026 entry-window
   dates** — the program's own site (appoftheyear.co.za) could not be
   loaded successfully across multiple attempts in this engagement
   (repeated timeouts), so exact 2026 entry-open/entry-close dates and
   the current total prize figure are unconfirmed beyond the pattern
   inferred from the 2025 cycle.
2. **No confirmed status of an open, earlier-stage Injini cohort in
   2026** — only the (eligibility-excluded) Mastercard Foundation
   Fellowship cycle was confirmed as a currently-tracked 2026 program;
   the earlier-stage, smaller-grant Injini track's current cohort status
   could not be found.
3. **TIA's fit for a non-research-affiliated, solo consumer-app founder
   remains a genuine open question**, not a resolved fit or exclusion —
   TIA's public materials are structured around Technology Readiness
   Levels and university/TTO-linked pathways, and no source directly
   addressed whether an independent solo mobile-app founder without a
   research-institution affiliation would qualify.
4. **SEDA Technology Programme's timeline could not be pinned to STP
   specifically** — the 4–12-week figure found applies to SEDA support
   programmes generally, not confirmed as STP's own cycle.
5. **Founder-specific eligibility facts remain unknown to this research**
   — age (for NYDA), race/gender (for Google Black Founders Fund and
   several 22 On Sloane / Grindstone women-focused programmes), and
   current company-registration status (for SEDA, and plausibly others).
   None of these were assumed in either direction; they gate several of
   the findings above and would need direct founder confirmation before
   any program is pursued.
