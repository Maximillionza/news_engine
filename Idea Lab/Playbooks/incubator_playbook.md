# Incubator Playbook

Accumulated process knowledge for the Incubator, across all case studies.
This is NOT case content — no idea names, industries, mechanics, or
identifying specifics belong here, even in generalized form. If a lesson
is specific enough that someone who knows the case portfolio could guess
which case it came from, it does not belong here.

Every entry below was proposed by the Incubator at the end of an
engagement and explicitly approved by the user before being added. The
Incubator does not write to this file itself.

## Entries

1. **Analytical fixes don't always move the Readiness Score — say so
   explicitly.** When a reviewing body's required changes are
   analytical/structural in nature (reconciling existing figures, naming
   a risk explicitly, precisely quantifying a known tension) rather than
   closing a genuine information gap, addressing them thoroughly can
   increase a case's rigor without moving its Readiness Score. This
   should be stated explicitly in the revision itself, rather than left
   for the next reviewing body to notice on its own, since an unchanged
   score after a "changes addressed" revision can otherwise be misread as
   the feedback having been ignored.
   *(Approved 2026-07-08, proposed during a case's Incubator revision
   following an Investment Committee "proceed with changes" verdict.)*

2. **A redesign of one mechanic's direction can silently break an
   unrelated feature that assumed the old direction — check for that
   before scoring the section Complete.** When a user clarification
   changes who bears a cost or who has visibility into something, treat
   it as a correction to every previously affected section, not an
   addition — and use that same pass to actively check whether any
   other, un-named feature in the case implicitly depended on the old
   direction. Redesigns of this kind tend to have ripple effects on
   adjacent mechanics that the clarification itself never mentions; a
   section should not be marked Complete again until any such ripple
   effect is either resolved or explicitly named as an open question.
   *(Approved 2026-07-08, proposed after a mechanic redesign surfaced an
   unrelated feature's dependency on the mechanic's old direction.)*

3. **Track Confidence trajectory as its own signal, separate from Status
   and the Readiness Score.** A Readiness Score built purely on Status
   can hold flat, or even look stable, while a section's Confidence
   quietly erodes across revisions. A Status-only rubric cannot
   distinguish "genuinely unchanged" from "more uncertain than it was
   two revisions ago" — surface a Confidence downgrade explicitly when it
   happens, particularly for Critical sections, rather than letting it
   pass unremarked inside an unchanged score. This gives the next
   reviewing body information the score alone would hide.
   *(Approved 2026-07-09, proposed after two Critical sections' Confidence
   dropped across consecutive revisions while the Readiness Score itself
   held flat.)*

4. **A rising Confidence tag can mean "clearer picture," not "better
   news" — state the two separately.** Confidence can legitimately rise
   when new research improves the quality of evidence behind an
   assessment, even when that same research points toward a less
   favorable substantive conclusion than before. Confidence-in-the-
   assessment and favorability-of-the-conclusion are two different
   dimensions; whenever a Confidence tag moves on the strength of new
   research, state both explicitly, since an upward-moving tag alone can
   be misread as "the situation is improving" when the more accurate
   read is "the picture is clearer, and less favorable than assumed."
   *(Approved 2026-07-09, proposed after a Confidence tag recovered from
   Low to Medium on the strength of research that substantiated, rather
   than dissolved, the underlying risk.)*

5. **A section carried forward as an "Unchanged from vN, not
   reproduced" pointer becomes unreadable to any reviewer restricted to
   the current document only — verify every section contains its own
   substantive text, not just a provenance pointer.** A pointer conceals
   how thin the underlying content actually is, so its Status/Confidence
   tags can go unverified for many revisions in a row. Check this at
   every revision, since pointers compound in a way that becomes very
   difficult to unwind once an intermediate version is no longer
   accessible to the reviewer doing the unwinding.
   *(Approved 2026-07-09, proposed after several sections were found to
   have degraded across multiple revisions into contentless pointers,
   discovered only when a downstream reviewing body would have been
   unable to read them.)*

6. **A reconstruction baseline should never be assumed complete merely
   because it does not present as a bare "unchanged" pointer.** Verify
   the baseline's own content against still-earlier versions before
   treating it as ground truth — content can be silently thinned through
   legitimate-looking rewrites, not only through pointer degradation,
   well before the version chosen as the recovery baseline. A single hop
   back is not the same as tracing to first divergence.
   *(Approved 2026-07-09, proposed after a first consolidation pass,
   built on a single assumed-good baseline version, was found to have
   missed content that had already been silently thinned even earlier in
   the version history.)*

7. **When new specialist evidence resolves the gap holding back one or
   more Critical sections, check each adjacent non-Critical section's
   own upgrade in the same cycle against whether that evidence was
   actually in scope for it.** Don't let sections drift upward on
   score-momentum from the Critical sections' resolution. A cycle that
   closes a long-standing blocker can create pressure — implicit or
   explicit — to also credit nearby sections that feel more resolved by
   association, even when the new evidence doesn't directly address
   their own named open items.
   *(Approved 2026-07-09, proposed after a cycle incorporating two
   genuine specialist opinions moved six sections to Complete in one
   pass, with the case's own Devil's Advocate later questioning whether
   all six were independently earned by the new evidence.)*

8. **When a rubric term's definition is tightened mid-case, state
   explicitly which sections of the prior version would have scored
   differently under the new definition before rescoring the current
   one.** This makes the tightening auditable and prevents any
   appearance that the definition was fitted to preserve the score.
   *(Approved 2026-07-09, proposed after the meaning of "Complete" was
   tightened in response to an Investment Committee integrity
   observation, and the revision stated which prior sections would have
   failed the new definition before applying it going forward.)*

9. **Candidate content drafted to satisfy a reviewing body's required
   change should never be scored as resolving the material question it
   stands in for.** However detailed or well-reasoned the draft, and
   however explicit the instruction to produce it, it should be scored
   identically to any other undecided specialist recommendation until
   the relevant specialist has actually reviewed it — the section
   carrying it should say so plainly rather than let the existence of a
   draft read as resolution.
   *(Approved 2026-07-09, proposed after a cycle drafted candidate
   content on two specialists' behalf to satisfy Investment Committee
   required changes, and explicitly declined to score either as
   resolved pending actual specialist review.)*

10. **When a reviewing body's integrity finding shows a prior score was
    not soundly earned under the rubric's own standard, state the
    honest prior-cycle baseline explicitly before presenting the current
    cycle's corrected score.** Never let a recovered score stand alone
    without showing what it recovered from — an unadorned "we now clear
    the threshold" reads identically whether the case improved or the
    scoring was merely corrected back to where it should have been.
    *(Approved 2026-07-09, proposed after a Gate Integrity Check showed a
    prior version's self-certified 70% score should have been 67.7% under
    its own stated standard, and the correcting cycle disclosed that
    honest baseline alongside its own recovered score.)*

11. **When a revision cycle resolves multiple required changes that are
    process/scheduling fixes or corrections to vague language, rather
    than closures of every material open question within a section,
    explicitly recompute and state whether the readiness score moved —
    and do not let the number of required changes resolved imply a
    proportional score increase.** A section's score only moves when
    every material question in its own scope is resolved; partial
    resolutions spread across many sections can legitimately net to zero
    score movement, and that should be reported as a plain, expected
    outcome rather than treated as an implicit shortfall.
    *(Approved 2026-07-09, proposed after a cycle resolved six of seven
    required changes without moving the Readiness Score, and stated
    plainly why rather than letting the flat score read as a failure.)*

12. **Before drafting a revision from scratch, check whether the target
    output path already contains content — a prior interrupted pass may
    have already produced a complete, correct revision.** Reading it
    first (which the write tooling requires regardless) can surface
    reusable, already-scrutinized work rather than triggering a
    redundant full rebuild; only discard it after verifying it actually
    meets the current delegation's instructions, not on the assumption
    that starting fresh is inherently safer.
    *(Approved 2026-07-09, proposed after a retried delegation found a
    prior attempt's interrupted-but-complete output already at the
    target path, verified it independently reached the correct
    conclusions, and adopted it rather than rebuilding from scratch.)*

13. **When a cycle resolves a previously-open item with an unfavorable
    finding — the fact becomes known but confirms rather than reduces
    the underlying risk — treat "resolved" and "de-risked" as two
    separate claims.** Recompute the readiness score under the
    strictest defensible reading of the affected section's own
    completion standard and disclose that computation explicitly, even
    if not adopted as the operative score, rather than letting stronger
    risk-language in the narrative substitute for any actual change in
    the number.
    *(Approved 2026-07-09, proposed after resolving a founder-eligibility
    question confirmed two funding candidates were excluded rather than
    validated, and the cycle disclosed an alternative, stricter score
    reading alongside the operative one.)*
